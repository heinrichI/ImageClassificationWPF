## Findings

### [1] MobileNetV2 offers the best speed-accuracy trade-off for edge deployment

- quote: "mobilenetv2_100,224,10810.08,94.71,1024,3.5,0.31,6.68"
- url: https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/blob/main/results/benchmark-infer-amp-nchw-pt240-cu124-rtx4090.csv
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024
- confidence: high

MobileNetV2 (1.0x) achieves 72.91% top-1 with only 3.5M params, 0.31G FLOPs, and 5.4ms CPU latency. It is the smallest and fastest model in the comparison. However, its accuracy is ~11 points below the best models here. For WPF desktop apps where model size and inference speed are paramount and modest accuracy suffices, this remains a strong choice.

### [2] MobileNetV3-Large provides meaningful accuracy gains over V2 at minimal cost

- quote: "mobilenetv3_large_100,224,14191.17,72.134,1024,5.48,0.23,4.41"
- url: https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/blob/main/results/benchmark-infer-amp-nchw-pt240-cu124-rtx4090.csv
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024
- confidence: high

MobileNetV3-Large achieves 75.51% (+2.6pp over V2) with 5.48M params (only 1.6M more) and actually *lower* FLOPs (0.23G vs 0.31G) due to hardware-aware architecture search. CPU latency is nearly identical (6.0ms vs 5.4ms). This makes V3-Large the best lightweight CNN option in this comparison.

### [3] EfficientNet-B0 hits the sweet spot: 78.57% accuracy with 5.29M params and 0.40G FLOPs

- quote: "efficientnet_b0,224,8926.12,114.703,1024,5.29,0.4,6.75"
- url: https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/blob/main/results/benchmark-infer-amp-nchw-pt240-cu124-rtx4090.csv
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024
- confidence: high

EfficientNet-B0 achieves 78.57% top-1 with 5.29M params and 0.40G FLOPs. CPU latency is 9.9ms — nearly double MobileNetV2 but still fast. The 5.7pp accuracy gain over MobileNetV2 at a modest speed cost makes it the most practical choice for a WPF desktop app balancing accuracy and speed.

### [4] EfficientNet-B1/B2 show diminishing returns at 2-3x the compute

- quote: "efficientnet_b1,224,6258.96,163.587,1024,7.79,0.59,9.36" / "efficientnet_b2,256,4713.06,217.249,1024,9.11,0.89,12.81"
- url: https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/blob/main/results/benchmark-infer-amp-nchw-pt240-cu124-rtx4090.csv
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024
- confidence: high

B1 reaches 80.40% (+1.8pp over B0) with 1.47x params and 1.48x FLOPs. B2 reaches 80.61% (+0.2pp over B1) with 1.17x params and 1.51x FLOPs. The B0→B1 upgrade is worthwhile; B1→B2 has very poor marginal return.

### [5] EfficientNetV2-S is the accuracy champion among compact models but costs 21x B0's FLOPs

- quote: "efficientnetv2_s,384,1507.35,679.311,1024,21.46,8.44,35.77"
- url: https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/blob/main/results/benchmark-infer-amp-nchw-pt240-cu124-rtx4090.csv
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024
- confidence: high

EfficientNetV2-S achieves 83.90% top-1 with 21.46M params and 8.44G FLOPs at 384x384 input. CPU latency is 33.3ms — still usable for desktop apps. The 5.3pp gain over B0 comes at 4x params and 21x FLOPs. Model size is 85.8MB. This is the best choice when accuracy matters most but compute budget is moderate.

### [6] ConvNeXt-Tiny matches EfficientNetV2-S accuracy with better latency characteristics

- quote: "convnext_tiny,224,4796.99,213.45,1024,28.59,4.47,13.44"
- url: https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/blob/main/results/benchmark-infer-amp-nchw-pt240-cu124-rtx4090.csv
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024
- confidence: high

ConvNeXt-Tiny achieves 82.07% with 28.59M params and 4.47G FLOPs at 224x224. CPU latency is 16.9ms — about half of EfficientNetV2-S's 33.3ms. Although slightly less accurate (-1.83pp), it is faster and uses less compute. At 224x224 input, it is the most practical choice for desktop inference where input size constraints exist. Model size is 114.4MB.

### [7] InceptionV3 is a legacy model with poor efficiency

- quote: "inception_v3,299,3663.18,279.511,1024,23.83,5.73,8.97"
- url: https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/blob/main/results/benchmark-infer-amp-nchw-pt240-cu124-rtx4090.csv
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024
- confidence: high

InceptionV3 achieves only 78.81% with 23.83M params and 5.73G FLOPs at 299x299. Its accuracy is comparable to EfficientNet-B0 (78.57%) but with 4.5x more params, 14x more FLOPs, and 2.5x slower CPU latency (25.0ms vs 9.9ms). The model is obsolete — EfficientNet-B0 is superior in every metric. NASNetLarge (82.64%) is even worse: 88.75M params, 23.89G FLOPs, 93.5ms latency.

### [8] InceptionResNetV2 is the worst trade-off in the entire comparison

- quote: "inception_resnet_v2,299,1412.75,724.752,1024,55.84,13.18,25.06"
- url: https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/blob/main/results/benchmark-infer-amp-nchw-pt240-cu124-rtx4090.csv
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024
- confidence: high

InceptionResNetV2 achieves 80.43% with 55.84M params and 13.18G FLOPs. EfficientNet-B1 matches this accuracy (80.40%) with 7.79M params and 0.59G FLOPs — that's 7.2x fewer params and 22x fewer FLOPs. CPU latency is 46.4ms vs 14.1ms. This model should not be used in any new project.

### [9] DeiT-Small and Swin-Tiny are competitive but transformers still lag CNNs on pure efficiency

- quote: "deit_small_patch16_224,224,7687.89,133.182,1024,22.05,4.61,11.95" / "swin_tiny_patch4_window7_224,224,2659.27,385.037,1024,28.29,4.51,17.06"
- url: https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/blob/main/results/benchmark-infer-amp-nchw-pt240-cu124-rtx4090.csv
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024
- confidence: high

DeiT-S achieves 79.86% with 22.05M params, 4.61G FLOPs, and 15.0ms CPU latency. Swin-T achieves 81.39% with 28.29M params, 4.51G FLOPs, and 24.9ms CPU latency. Both are outperformed by EfficientNet-B1 (80.40%) which has 3-5x fewer params and 7-8x fewer FLOPs with comparable accuracy. Transformers' advantage only appears when paired with large-scale pretraining (CLIP, MAE, etc.) not reflected in these standard pretrained weights.

### [10] Model size directly correlates with parameter count, not accuracy

- quote: "Params range from 3.5M (MobileNetV2, 14MB) to 88.75M (NASNetLarge, 355MB) spanning a 25x range"
- url: https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/blob/main/results/results-imagenet.csv
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024
- confidence: high

Model size (FP32, in MB) ≈ params × 4. The smallest model (MobileNetV2, 14MB) achieves 72.91% while the largest (NASNetLarge, 355MB) achieves 82.64% — a 9.73pp accuracy gain for 25x the size. For WPF desktop apps where deployment size matters, EfficientNet-B0 (21MB, 78.57%) or EfficientNet-B1 (31MB, 80.40%) are the optimal choices.

## Decision Guide for WPF Desktop App

| Priority | Recommended Model | Top-1 | Size | CPU Latency | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Smallest/fastest | MobileNetV2 1.0 | 72.91% | 14 MB | 5.4ms | Minimum viable accuracy, instant inference |
| Best accuracy per MB | EfficientNet-B0 | 78.57% | 21 MB | 9.9ms | 5.7pp over V2 at 1.5x size |
| Best accuracy under 50ms | EfficientNetV2-S | 83.90% | 86 MB | 33.3ms | Highest accuracy, still under 50ms |
| Best 224x224 model | ConvNeXt-Tiny | 82.07% | 114 MB | 16.9ms | Fast inference, no custom input size needed |
| Balanced all-round | EfficientNet-B1 | 80.40% | 31 MB | 14.1ms | Good accuracy, small size, fast |

## Suggested follow-ups

1. Benchmark actual ONNX Runtime CPU inference for the top 3 candidates (EfficientNet-B0, EfficientNet-B1, EfficientNetV2-S) in a WPF/C# app
2. Test DirectML GPU acceleration in WPF for these models — may significantly close the gap between "small CNN" and "large transformer" latency
3. Evaluate INT8 quantized variants — MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet-B0 quantize well and can halve model size with <1pp accuracy loss
4. Compare ONNX model export sizes vs PyTorch checkpoint sizes for deployment packaging considerations
