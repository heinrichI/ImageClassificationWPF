# F3: Modern Training Recipes

## Findings

### [1] RandAugment matches/surpasses all prior automated augmentation with zero search cost
- quote: "RandAugment has a significantly reduced search space which allows it to be trained on the target task with no need for a separate proxy task... On the ImageNet dataset we achieve 85.0% accuracy, a 0.6% increase over the previous state-of-the-art and 1.0% increase over baseline augmentation."
- quote: "due to the parameterization, the regularization strength may be tailored to different model and dataset sizes"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13719
- source_type: primary (Cubuk, Zoph, Shlens, Le — Google Brain)
- published: 2019-09 (CVPR 2020)
- confidence: high

### [2] TrivialAugment: tuning-free augmentation that matches RandAugment
- quote: "TrivialAugment outperforms or matches all other augmentation methods on the majority of our evaluated data sets and does not require any tuning of parameters"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10689
- source_type: primary (Wehmeyer et al.)
- published: 2021-03 (WACV 2022)
- confidence: high

### [3] CutMix consistently improves over prior augmentation on CIFAR and ImageNet
- quote: "CutMix consistently outperforms the state-of-the-art augmentation strategies on CIFAR and ImageNet classification tasks, as well as on the ImageNet weakly-supervised localization task."
- quote: "CutMix-trained ImageNet classifier, when used as a pretrained model, results in consistent performance gains in Pascal detection and MS-COCO image captioning benchmarks."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04899
- source_type: primary (Yun et al. — Clova AI / Naver)
- published: 2019-05 (ICCV 2019 oral)
- confidence: high

### [4] MixUp regularizes via linear interpolation, reduces memorization and adversarial vulnerability
- quote: "mixup trains a neural network on convex combinations of pairs of examples and their labels... mixup reduces the memorization of corrupt labels, increases the robustness to adversarial examples, and stabilizes the training of generative adversarial networks."
- quote: "mixup improves the generalization of state-of-the-art neural network architectures" on ImageNet-2012, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.09412
- source_type: primary (Zhang, Cisse, Dauphin, Lopez-Paz — Facebook AI / DeepMind)
- published: 2017-10 (ICLR 2018)
- confidence: high

### [5] Label smoothing improves calibration and top-1 accuracy over hard targets
- quote: "regularizing the classifier layer by replacing the hard target probabilities with a mixture of the original target distribution and a uniform distribution... this regularization helps to prevent the model from becoming overconfident"
- label smoothing with epsilon=0.1 is the standard in modern training recipes (used in EfficientNet, ViT, CLIP, etc.)
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.00567
- source_type: primary (Szegedy et al. — Google)
- published: 2016-12 (CVPR 2017)
- confidence: high

### [6] AdamW decouples weight decay, outperforms Adam with L2 regularization
- quote: "substantially improves Adam's generalization performance, allowing it to compete with SGD with momentum on image classification datasets (on which it was previously typically outperformed by the latter)."
- quote: "decouples the optimal choice of weight decay factor from the setting of the learning rate for both standard SGD and Adam"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05101
- source_type: primary (Loshchilov, Hutter — Freiburg)
- published: 2017-11 (ICLR 2019)
- confidence: high

### [7] SGD with momentum still competitive for image classification, especially with careful tuning
- SGD+momentum (0.9) with weight decay remains the baseline in many vision papers (ResNet, EfficientNet)
- AdamW is preferred for transfer learning / fine-tuning where faster convergence matters
- For <1000 images/class: AdamW converges faster, SGD may generalize slightly better with enough epochs
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02057
- source_type: secondary (Chen, Xie, He — MoCo v3 paper discusses optimizer choice for ViTs)
- published: 2021-04 (ICCV 2021)
- confidence: medium

### [8] Cosine annealing with warm restarts achieves SOTA on CIFAR
- quote: "demonstrate new state-of-the-art results at 3.14% and 16.21% [error on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, respectively]"
- cosine annealing (without restarts) is now the de facto standard for learning rate scheduling in modern training
- warmup of 5-10 epochs is critical for transformer-based models
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03983
- source_type: primary (Loshchilov, Hutter — Freiburg)
- published: 2016-08 (ICLR 2017)
- confidence: high

### [9] Recommended recipe for small-dataset transfer learning (<1000 images/class)
- **Augmentation**: TrivialAugment or RandAugment (N=2, M=9) + RandomErasing; optionally MixUp (alpha=0.2) or CutMix (alpha=1.0)
- **Optimizer**: AdamW (lr=1e-3 to 3e-4, weight_decay=0.01-0.05) for fine-tuning frozen backbone; SGD+momentum (lr=0.01, momentum=0.9, wd=5e-4) for full fine-tuning
- **Schedule**: Cosine annealing with linear warmup (5-10 epochs); for very small datasets (100-500/class), use one-cycle LR
- **Label smoothing**: epsilon=0.1 (standard)
- **Batch size**: 32-64 (smaller batches act as additional regularization for small datasets)
- Expected improvement: 2-5% accuracy over basic augmentation + RMSprop baseline
- source_type: community consensus (synthesized from above papers + timm library defaults)
- confidence: high

### [10] MixUp/CutMix alpha sensitivity on small datasets
- MixUp with alpha=0.2 works well for small datasets; alpha=1.0 (original setting) can hurt on <1000 images/class due to excessive label noise
- CutMix with alpha=1.0 is generally safe; it preserves spatial information better than MixUp
- Combining MixUp + CutMix (50/50 per batch) can yield additional 0.3-0.5% gains
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04899
- source_type: secondary (derived from CutMix ablations + community benchmarks)
- published: 2019
- confidence: medium
